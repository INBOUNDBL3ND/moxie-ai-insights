MOXIE's Analysis

Civic communication is at its best when it is useful, and July filled Ellisville's channels with exactly that kind of practical, community-serving work. The centerpiece was a commercial trash-collection resident survey the team developed, distributed, and monitored, gathering 28 responses so far with a reminder email already scheduled to lift that number. Print played a role too: the coupon marketing booklet went to press for a run of 100 copies, putting local business offers directly into residents' hands. On social, the team posted a local ribbon cutting and a paint-recycling event, keeping neighbors current on what is happening around town. The website saw real upkeep as well, with every location updated to carry the new renewal form and a business-spotlight video added to showcase one of the city's own. The regular newsletter kept residents and the business community connected on a direct cadence, while ongoing posts and site maintenance held their steady rhythm. July reinforced a simple idea: when the city's channels answer the questions people actually have, they become the first place the community looks.
